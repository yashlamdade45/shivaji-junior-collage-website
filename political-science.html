Practice Question Bank

Study practice: These original revision questions are not official board questions. Open each answer to self-check and use constitutional or institutional examples from your text.

  1. Name four features commonly found in a democratic constitution.
    Check answer

    Examples include defined institutions and powers, rule of law, protection of rights, elections or representative government, and procedures for amendment. Constitutions differ, so use the features relevant to the example in the question.

  2. How are citizens' rights and duties related?
    Check answer

    Rights protect freedoms and enable participation; duties describe responsibilities toward others and the community. Duties should not be used to cancel rights, and both are shaped by the constitution and law.

  3. Give one difference between parliamentary and presidential systems.
    Check answer

    In a parliamentary system, the executive is drawn from and accountable to the legislature. In a presidential system, the executive is institutionally separate from the legislature and does not depend on legislative confidence in the same way.

  4. What role can pressure groups play in a democracy?
    Check answer

    They organise people with shared interests, inform public debate and seek to influence policy. They do not normally contest elections to form the government, and their influence can vary.

  5. Name two ways international organisations can support peace.
    Check answer

    They can provide forums for negotiation and mediation, support peacekeeping under agreed mandates, and coordinate humanitarian assistance. Their success depends on member-state cooperation and their authority.

  6. What is federalism?
    Check answer

    Federalism constitutionally divides governmental powers between a national government and regional units, with each level having authority in its assigned areas.

  7. Why are free and fair elections important?
    Check answer

    They let people choose representatives, provide a peaceful way to change government and make leaders answerable to voters. Fairness requires meaningful competition and protection against coercion or fraud.

  8. Distinguish a state from a government.
    Check answer

    A state is a continuing political entity with territory, population, government and sovereignty. A government is the set of institutions and officeholders that administers the state and can change over time.
