Practice Question Bank

Study practice: These original revision questions are not official board questions. Open each answer to self-check and state assumptions when explaining economic relationships.

  1. Distinguish between microeconomics and macroeconomics.
    Check answer

    Microeconomics studies individual consumers, firms and markets. Macroeconomics studies economy-wide measures and issues such as total output, inflation and unemployment.

  2. What usually happens to demand for a good when the price of one of its substitutes rises?
    Check answer

    Other things equal, demand for the good tends to increase because consumers may switch from the now more expensive substitute. This is a shift in the demand curve, not merely movement along it.

  3. Give two ways agriculture contributes to India's economy.
    Check answer

    It supplies food and raw materials, and provides livelihoods and employment. It also creates demand for goods and services and links to food processing and trade.

  4. State two objectives and one limitation of a government budget.
    Check answer

    Objectives may include allocating resources, redistributing income, supporting stability or promoting growth. A limitation is that forecasts can be uncertain; implementation and fiscal constraints can also affect outcomes.

  5. Name two measures that can help reduce unemployment.
    Check answer

    Measures include skills and vocational training linked to demand, and supporting job-creating investment or labour-intensive sectors. Explain that effectiveness depends on local conditions and implementation.

  6. What is opportunity cost?
    Check answer

    It is the value of the next-best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

  7. If a market price is above equilibrium, what pressure tends to move it toward equilibrium?
    Check answer

    At a price above equilibrium, quantity supplied tends to exceed quantity demanded, creating a surplus. Sellers face pressure to reduce price, other things equal.

  8. How does a general rise in prices differ from a rise in the price of one good?
    Check answer

    Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level across an economy. A change in one good's price alone is a relative price change and does not by itself establish inflation.
