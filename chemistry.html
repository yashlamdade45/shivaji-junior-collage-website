Practice Question Bank

Study practice: These original revision questions are not official board questions. Open each answer to self-check, then compare key terms with your prescribed text.

  1. Name four factors that can change the rate of a chemical reaction.
    Check answer

    Concentration of reactants, temperature, surface area of a solid reactant, and the presence of a catalyst are common factors. For gases, pressure can also affect rate by changing concentration.

  2. Compare ionic and covalent bonding.
    Check answer

    Ionic bonding involves electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions after electron transfer. Covalent bonding involves atoms sharing one or more pairs of electrons.

  3. What is a polymer? Give one example and its monomer.
    Check answer

    A polymer is a large molecule made of repeating units. Polyethene is an addition polymer formed from ethene molecules.

  4. In Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu, identify what is oxidised and reduced.
    Check answer

    Zinc is oxidised: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻. Copper(II) ions are reduced: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. The equation is balanced as written.

  5. For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), which way does equilibrium shift when pressure increases?
    Check answer

    It shifts to the right, toward ammonia, because that side has fewer moles of gas (two versus four), assuming temperature is unchanged.

  6. Define molarity and state its usual unit.
    Check answer

    Molarity is the amount of solute in moles per litre of solution. Its usual unit is mol L⁻¹ (or mol dm⁻³).

  7. What is the effect of a catalyst on activation energy and equilibrium position?
    Check answer

    A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy and speeds up both forward and reverse reactions. It does not change the equilibrium constant or equilibrium position.

  8. Distinguish a galvanic cell from an electrolytic cell.
    Check answer

    A galvanic cell uses a spontaneous redox reaction to produce electrical energy. An electrolytic cell uses an external electrical supply to drive a non-spontaneous reaction.
