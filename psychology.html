Practice Question Bank

Study practice: These original revision questions are not official board questions. Open each answer to self-check; describe theories accurately and use examples to explain them.

  1. Distinguish sensation from perception.
    Check answer

    Sensation is detecting stimuli through sensory receptors. Perception is organising and interpreting sensory information to make meaning of it.

  2. Briefly describe Maslow's hierarchy of needs.
    Check answer

    Maslow proposed levels commonly shown as physiological, safety, love/belonging, esteem and self-actualisation needs. The model suggests lower needs can become more pressing before higher growth needs; do not treat it as a rigid rule for every person.

  3. Name two evidence-informed ways to support attention and memory while studying.
    Check answer

    Use spaced study sessions and active retrieval (try recalling without looking, then check). Reducing distractions and taking adequate breaks can also support sustained attention.

  4. What is emotional intelligence, and how can it help relationships?
    Check answer

    It refers broadly to recognising and understanding emotions and responding to them thoughtfully. It can support empathy, communication and conflict management, but does not mean suppressing emotions.

  5. Name two ways psychological support can promote resilience.
    Check answer

    Support can help people develop coping skills and connect with social resources. Appropriate professional care can also help identify needs and plan support; resilience does not mean avoiding difficulty or never needing help.

  6. In classical conditioning, what is the difference between an unconditioned and a conditioned stimulus?
    Check answer

    An unconditioned stimulus naturally elicits a response without prior learning. A conditioned stimulus, after being paired with it, comes to elicit a learned response.

  7. How does negative reinforcement differ from punishment?
    Check answer

    Negative reinforcement removes an unpleasant condition to increase a behaviour. Punishment is intended to decrease a behaviour. “Negative” means removal, not necessarily something bad.

  8. Give one difference between working memory and long-term memory.
    Check answer

    Working memory briefly holds and manipulates information currently in use, with limited capacity. Long-term memory stores information over longer periods and has much greater capacity.
