Practice Question Bank

Study practice: These original revision questions are not official board questions. Open each answer to self-check, then compare key terms with your prescribed text.

  1. Name the main stages of mitosis and state one event in each.
    Check answer

    Prophase: chromosomes condense; metaphase: chromosomes align at the equator; anaphase: sister chromatids separate; telophase: new nuclei form. Cytokinesis then divides the cytoplasm.

  2. How do enzymes increase the rate of metabolic reactions?
    Check answer

    Enzymes bind specific substrates at an active site and lower the reaction's activation energy. They are not consumed, and their activity depends on conditions such as temperature and pH.

  3. Give two differences between innate and acquired immunity.
    Check answer

    Innate immunity is present from birth and responds broadly. Acquired immunity develops after exposure to an antigen, is specific, and can form memory cells for a faster later response.

  4. What does “semi-conservative” DNA replication mean?
    Check answer

    Each daughter DNA molecule contains one original parental strand and one newly synthesised complementary strand.

  5. State one agricultural and one medical application of biotechnology.
    Check answer

    Agriculture: tissue culture can rapidly produce many plants with desired traits. Medicine: recombinant DNA methods can produce human insulin. Give the process and application clearly in a longer response.

  6. How does meiosis differ from mitosis in chromosome number and outcome?
    Check answer

    Meiosis involves two divisions and usually produces four genetically varied haploid cells from one diploid cell. Mitosis involves one division and produces two genetically similar daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the parent.

  7. Distinguish pollination from fertilisation in flowering plants.
    Check answer

    Pollination is transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. Fertilisation is fusion of the male and female gametes, usually in the ovule, forming a zygote.

  8. What happens to energy as it moves through a food chain?
    Check answer

    Energy flows from producers to consumers and less is available at each successive trophic level because organisms use much of it for life processes and lose it as heat. Matter, unlike energy, is recycled.
